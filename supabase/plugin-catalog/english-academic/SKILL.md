# English academic translation

## Scope
- Use when the project's translation language is English and the readers are scholars.

## Core method
- Read the entire source and its context before composing a complete English translation.
- Use confirmed punctuation when supplied; otherwise interpret the unpunctuated source yourself. Never require the user to punctuate or segment first.
- Preserve all source meaning, rhetorical relationships, names, dates and qualifications. Do not summarize or silently omit difficult passages.
- Organize paragraphs by meaning and rhetorical flow, independently of source line breaks. Preserve verse lines and enumerations.
- Follow the supplied terminology, Hanja, supplementation and uncertainty policies. Distinguish uncertain readings and supplied explanations from source evidence; never invent certainty.

## Names and terms
- Romanize Korean personal names, place names, offices and book titles with McCune–Reischauer, consistently. If the project glossary gives a form, use it instead. (Edit this line if your project uses Revised Romanization.)
- Use pinyin for Chinese people, places, dynasties and titles.
- Keep the source order of names (family name first). Keep courtesy names, pen names and posthumous titles as they appear; do not silently replace them with another name.
- Translate offices and institutions on first occurrence and add the romanized form in parentheses, for example "Chief State Councilor (Yŏngŭijŏng)". Use the same English form afterwards.
- Give book titles in romanized form followed by an English translation in parentheses on first occurrence.

## Dates
- Keep reign-year dating. Add the Western year in parentheses only when the conversion is certain, for example "the third year of King T'aejong's reign (1403)".
- Keep lunar months and days as lunar dates ("the fifth day of the third month"); do not convert them to Gregorian dates.
- Romanize sexagenary day names, for example "the kapcha day".

## Style
- Use formal academic English and the past tense for narration.
- Render a memorialist's humble self-reference (臣) as "your subject" where it marks deference to the ruler; otherwise use "I".
- The translation is plain text. Do not use Markdown or italics; the romanized form marks a foreign term.
- Do not add explanations to the running text; follow the supplementation policy.

## Review before submitting
- Check the translation against the source for omissions, additions and inconsistent romanization.
- Check that each office, person and title uses one English form throughout.
